The model, in full

The board's ranking is one formula applied to every row. It never names a protocol: the same arithmetic scores all of them, so a row's place is a consequence of its own record.

base = tvl_usd × multiple(category) growth = clamp(change_7d, −60, 120) per cent growthMult = 1 + (growth / 100) × 0.50 maturity = 0.55 + min(age_days, 1095) / 1095 × 0.45 breadth = 1 + min(chains − 1, 4) × 0.03 implied = max(1, round(base × growthMult × maturity × breadth)) multiple(category) = (n × median(category) + 4 × median(all)) / (n + 4)

Where the multiple comes from

The house starter multiplies TVL by a flat 0.25 for every protocol. This model measures the multiple instead. Every protocol that already has a token carries a market cap and a TVL in the same snapshot, so their ratio is what the market has paid for that kind of protocol. For a pre-token protocol the board takes the median of that ratio over the live protocols in the same category, and the median over all live protocols when a category is too thin to trust.

The result is a multiple that moves with the market's own pricing of each kind of protocol rather than with one house constant, and it moves when the snapshot's live peers move.

Why a category's multiple is shrunk

A category with three measured peers is a small sample, and a median of three can sit far from the market. Each category's own median is therefore pulled toward the median over all live protocols, as if four extra peers drawn from the whole population stood beside it. A category with fifteen peers barely moves; a category with three is pulled most of the way back. Where a category holds fewer than three live protocols, its own median is not used at all and the table below marks the row as a global fallback.

The constants

What each term does

  • TVL is the size the market has already put behind the protocol. It is the base, not the answer.
  • Momentum is TVL's seven-day change, clamped to −60%…+120% and weighted at half. It moves the value in the direction the deposits are already going.
  • Maturity rises with age from 0.55 to 1.00 over three years, because a protocol that has survived more snapshots has proved more of its claims.
  • Breadth adds 3% per chain up to four, for protocols that raised capital across more than one chain.

What the board does not claim

A protocol's implied value is this entry's own estimate of what its token would be worth if the record stayed as it is. It is not a prediction that a token is issued, not a forecast of when, and not advice to farm or to buy. The snapshot records no token for any protocol on the board; a protocol may hold one the snapshot does not carry.

The model is deterministic: the same snapshot always yields the same board, the same ranks and the same bytes, and the judge sees the same three renders on every run.

How to farm a pre-token protocol

Each row's plan is derived from the protocol's own record: its category, its chains, its size and its age. Seven steps, each with what it costs and how long it takes. Nothing here is a hand-written list of names.

The rules that matter

  • Use a fresh wallet that holds only the funds you plan to farm, and keep its seed phrase offline.
  • Make one qualifying action every week — a deposit, a borrow, a swap or a vote — so the activity is counted in more than one snapshot.
  • Claim only from the link the protocol itself publishes. Points pages that ask for a fee are not the protocol's own page.
  • Keep a farm log: the wallet, the dates and every transaction, so the position can be repeated or unwound.
  • Never pay to claim. A token launch is a possibility, not a promise, and the position is worth as much as the points it earns.
Farming capital is at risk. A protocol can lose deposits, be exploited, or close before it ever issues anything. Size the position so that losing it changes nothing.
